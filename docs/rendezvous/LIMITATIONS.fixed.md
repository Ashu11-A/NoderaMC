| L-23 | Cross-NAT direct P2P was unproven. The original plan was a jvm-libp2p transport that was **never built**; it was superseded by the standalone Rust rendezvous relay | The service speaks the frozen rendezvous/relay family: signed-record registration (Ed25519, TTL, trust-on-first-use identity binding, per-IP quota), paged discovery, HMAC relay reservations, and a tokio circuit bridge that meters bytes, duration, and idle time and tears down with a reason code. `RendezvousRelayIT` spawns the **real binary** and drives two relay-only Java peers through it: they register, discover each other, and a `PeerJoin` plus `SessionKeepAlive` cross the end-to-end-encrypted circuit byte-exact; exhausting the reservation's byte ceiling tears the circuit down; and the selector reports the direct path when one is available | [1](Task.1.md) | 2026-07-19 |
| L-27 | The direct socket transport needed reachable listen endpoints (LAN, port forwarding, or a VPN); there was no hole punching and no relay fallback | `RendezvousPeerTransport` composes direct-first and relay-fallback behind the **same** `PeerTransport` seam, with `SocketPeerTransport` remaining the LAN path. An X25519-ECDH + Ed25519-authenticated + AES-GCM `EndToEndCipher` means the relay forwards opaque bytes it can neither read nor forge, and a `TransportSelector` prefers direct over punched over relayed per (peer, message class), keeping bulk stream chunks off relays. A later audit found and fixed the structural half of this claim: the transport had advertised **only** a RELAY candidate, so direct-first was unreachable and every byte crossed the relay — it now publishes a HOST candidate from the direct transport's listen route and renews registration at half the TTL | [2](Task.2.md) | 2026-07-19 |
| L-83 | A drain's grace period could expire with circuits still bridged, and those circuits were then cut — bounded and reported, but the transfer a cut circuit was carrying was lost rather than moved | Writing the exit test first, as the row's own last trace proposed, found the one part that did **not** compose. A cut circuit and a departed peer reach the content plane as the *same* `onPeerDown`, and `PieceDownloader.onHolderLost` deleted the peer's holdings — so after a drain the download kept every piece it had verified and had **nobody left to ask** for the rest, even though the peer had merely migrated to the replacement relay and the next request would have re-dialled it (the transport tries every endpoint it knows). The fix is the smallest thing that closes it and needs no wire change: a lost holder's holdings are *remembered* rather than discarded, and the caller's existing stall nudge — `retryPending()`, which `WorldArchiveService` already calls every couple of seconds on a quiet fetch — restores them once. A peer that really is gone costs one skipped round; a peer that migrated costs a re-dial. Exit test green: `RelayDrainResumeIT.aCircuitCutWhenTheGracePeriodExpiresResumesThroughTheReplacementRelay`, in which a real `ContentTransferService` pair transfers a real manifest, the grace period expires mid-transfer, the bridged circuit is cut, and the **same** downloader completes through the replacement relay having re-fetched none of the pieces it already held. It is load-bearing: with the restoration removed the test fails, and its negative twin (`aDrainWithNoHolderRestorationLeavesTheTransferStuck`) shows the un-nudged download stuck at the cut. Unit half: `PieceDownloaderTest.aHolderLostToACutCircuitIsAskedAgainOnTheNextRetryRound`. **Scope of the proof:** the content plane, downloader, manifests and piece verification are real; the relay fabric is modelled in-JVM (same-relay-or-no-circuit, drain = migrate both ends then cut), because the real-binary end of the same three seams is already covered by `RendezvousRelayIT` and a Java unit-test lane should not depend on Rust release artifacts | [5](Task.5.md) | 2026-07-28 |
